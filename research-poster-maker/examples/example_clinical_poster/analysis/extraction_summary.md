### 1. Research title
Early SGLT2 Inhibitor Initiation Within 72 Hours of Acute Decompensated Heart Failure Admission and 24-Month Cardiorenal Outcomes: A Synthetic Multicenter Cohort Study

### 2. Authors and affiliations
Elena Rostova, MD, PhD; Marcus Vance, MD; Tariq Al-Mansoor, MPH; Hannah Lindqvist, PhD | Affiliations: Department of Cardiovascular Medicine & Biostatistics Collaborative, Synthetic Academic Health Network (SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DATA)

### 3. Background
Hospitalization for acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) carries high post-discharge mortality and worsening renal function. However, real-world effectiveness of initiating sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) within 72 hours of stabilization across broad ejection fraction phenotypes remains uncertain.

### 4. Research gap
However, real-world effectiveness of initiating sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) within 72 hours of stabilization across broad ejection fraction phenotypes remains uncertain.

### 5. Objectives
To evaluate whether early in-hospital SGLT2i initiation (<=72 h) versus delayed/standard post-discharge care is associated with lower 24-month cardiovascular death, HF rehospitalization, or >=40% sustained eGFR decline.

### 6. Study design
Multicenter Cohort Study

### 7. Methods
We conducted a prospective multicenter observational cohort study across 19 tertiary hospitals (N = 4,820 adults hospitalized for ADHF; early SGLT2i n = 2,410, standard care n = 2,410 after active-comparator overlap propensity weighting). Statistical analysis used overlap-weighted Cox proportional hazards models and Fine-Gray competing-risk models.

### 13. Important findings
Primary outcome occurred in 21.4% (516/2,410) of early SGLT2i patients versus 29.8% (718/2,410) in standard care (adjusted HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.56–0.82, p = 0.0001). Heart failure rehospitalization decreased significantly (aHR 0.65, 95% CI 0.53–0.80, p = 0.0002), and sustained >=40% eGFR decline was lower (aHR 0.71, 95% CI 0.55–0.92, p = 0.009). Ketoacidosis rates were low and comparable (0.4% vs 0.3%, p = 0.56).
